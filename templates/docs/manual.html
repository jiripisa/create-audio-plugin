Getting started

  1. Download the disk image from the download page and open it.
  2. Drag {{APP}} to Applications and start it from there.
  3. Click a key of the keyboard at the bottom of the window. Near the top of a key the note is soft, further down it is firmer. A MIDI keyboard plays it too: the app listens to every connected MIDI device until you choose one.
  4. The sound goes to the macOS output. To choose another device, or one MIDI device or channel, click the gear at the right of the header (Settings; see Settings).
  5. To play {{APP_NAME}} in a host such as Logic Pro or Ableton Live, open Settings and click Install Audio Unit (see The Audio Unit plug-in).

The app remembers the sound between launches: the keyboard's knobs, all sixteen pads, the mode, the selected pad and the preset each sound came from. It also keeps your MIDI controllers (see MIDI controllers).

Knobs and switches

A knob 1 shows its name above it and its value below it; the arc shows where it stands. Drag a knob up or down: 200 points of travel turn it from end to end. Hold Shift while dragging for steps ten times finer. Double-click a knob to take it back to its value in the preset the sound came from or, without one, in the program last loaded. A switch 2 shows its choices side by side; click one. A switch with more than four choices, such as the LFO shapes, is a menu.

Each control below is given with its range and its default, the value it has before a program or a preset changes it. A level knob whose range ends in “Off” is silent at the bottom of its travel.

Master

The knobs every sound has, whatever it is.

Volume (−40 to +6 dB, default 0.0 dB)
The level of the sound.
Pan (−100 to +100 %, default 0 %)
Left or right in the stereo output. Useful for placing pads apart.
Tune (−100 to +100 ct, default 0 ct)
Fine tuning in cents.
Octave (−2 to +2, default 0)
Moves the sound by octaves. At 0 a note sounds at its own pitch: A4 (note 69) is 440 Hz. Programs leave this knob where it is.
Bend Up, Bend Down (0–24 st, default 2 st)
How far the pitch bend wheel bends the pitch at its ends, up and down, in semitones (see Keyboard mode).
Voice Mode (Poly, Mono, Legato; default Poly)
How the keyboard plays notes that overlap. Poly: every key a voice of its own. Mono: one voice, the last key pressed. Legato: one voice that ties overlapping notes together without a new attack. See Voice modes, glide and sustain.
Glide (0–2000 ms, default 0 ms)
In Mono and Legato, how long the pitch takes to slide from one note to the next. At 0 it jumps. See Voice modes, glide and sustain.
LFO Start (Hit, Free; default Hit)
Hit starts both LFOs anew with every note, so the same note always moves the knobs the same way. Free lets them run on, and each note joins them where they are.
LFO 1 Rate, LFO 2 Rate (0.02–20 Hz, defaults 6.00 Hz and 2.00 Hz)
How often each LFO goes round. What an LFO moves, and how far, is set on the knobs themselves (see LFO and VEL amounts).
LFO 1 Shape, LFO 2 Shape (Sine, Triangle, Ramp Up, Ramp Down, Square, Steps, Wander; default Sine)
The shape of each LFO's cycle. Sine and Triangle sway; the ramps rise or fall and jump back; Square switches between two places; Steps takes a new random place every cycle and holds it; Wander takes one and moves there smoothly.
Vel Sens (0–100 %, default 70 %)
How much the velocity changes the level of a note: a softer note is quieter by the factor 1 − Vel Sens × (1 − velocity), where velocity runs from 0 to 1. At 0 every note is as loud as the hardest.

Keyboard mode

Every MIDI note, and every key on the screen, plays the keyboard's sound at the note's pitch, as Voice Mode says (see Voice modes, glide and sustain). That sound is what the knobs show in this mode.

  • A sounding note follows the knobs: turn one and you hear the change at once.
  • Sixteen voices sound at most, pads included. A note that would be the seventeenth stops the quietest voice within 3 ms.
Velocity
Sets the note's level as Vel Sens says and moves the knobs that have a VEL amount (see LFO and VEL amounts).
Pitch bend
Bends the pitch, by up to Bend Up semitones upward and Bend Down downward, in every voice mode.
Mod wheel (CC 1)
Aftertouch (channel pressure)

The wheels act on the keyboard's sound only; pads do not follow them, nor the pitch bend. Reset All Controllers (CC 121) puts the wheels and the bend back to rest and the sustain pedal up, and All Sound Off and All Notes Off (CC 120 and 123) fade every voice out within 10 ms.

Other MIDI controllers turn the knobs and switches themselves (see MIDI controllers).

Voice modes, glide and sustain

Voice Mode and Glide, in Master, say how the keyboard plays notes that overlap; they belong to the sound, like every knob. The sustain pedal holds notes in every voice mode. Pads ignore all three (see Pad mode).

Poly
Every key plays a voice of its own, so chords sound, up to sixteen notes at once.
Mono
One voice, the last key pressed. Every new note starts afresh, with its own attack, and the note it replaces fades out within 3 ms. Let go of the newest key while others are still held and the voice goes back to the newest of them without a new attack, as one note moving to another pitch; let go of the last key and the note ends.
Legato
As Mono, but a note played while another key is still held takes the voice over without a new attack: overlapping notes are tied into one line. A note played with no key held starts afresh. A tied note keeps the velocity and the LFOs of the note it took over.
Glide
How long the pitch takes to slide from where the voice is to the new note: always the same time, however far the notes are apart, at an even speed in semitones. Mono glides to every new note while its voice still sounds (held, held by the pedal, or ringing out); Legato glides only between overlapping notes; both glide when the voice goes back to a key still held. At 0 ms, in Poly and on the pads nothing glides.
Sustain pedal (CC 64)
While the pedal is down (64 or more), letting go of a key does not end its note; when the pedal comes up, the notes whose keys are up end, as if they had been let go just then. In Mono and Legato the keys still decide the pitch: letting go of a key while others are held still goes back to them, and the pedal holds only the last note. The pedal holds no pad: a pad's note ends when the pad is let go.
  • The keys held are remembered in every voice mode, the last sixteen of them, so a voice mode changed while keys are down finds them. Notes that sound when the voice mode changes go on until their keys are let go.
  • The sustain pedal is MIDI's own controller: no knob can learn CC 64 (see MIDI controllers).

Pad mode

In pad mode 5 the MIDI notes 36–51 (C2–D♯3) hit pads 1–16: note 36 is pad 1, note 37 pad 2, and so on, as on an MPC and most pad controllers. Other notes are ignored. Each pad holds a sound of its own, frozen with it, and plays it at a pitch of its own, whatever note hits it.

1The pads
Pad 1 is at the top left and the pads run row by row. Each shows its number, and its name above its pitch and its group (“C3 G1”; “Off” when it has none). A pad's name is that of the preset its sound came from or, without one, that of its program. A pad lights up when it is hit, from the screen or from MIDI.
2Selecting and playing a pad
Click a pad to play it and select it; the selected pad has an outline. As on the keys, near the top the hit is soft and further down it is firmer. The pad's note lasts until you let go of the mouse button.
Editing a pad
The knobs show the selected pad's sound, and turning them changes that pad only. 4 Program and Preset load into the selected pad. The keyboard's sound is left alone, and so are the other pads.
3Pitch and group
Beside the pads: which pad is selected, its pitch and its group (see below), and the Kit menu (see Kits).

A pad's note uses the pad's sound as it stood when the pad was hit: turning a knob changes the next hit, not one that sounds. The velocity moves the pad's knobs by the pad's own VEL amounts (see LFO and VEL amounts). Voice Mode, Glide, the sustain pedal, the pitch bend and the wheels do not act on pads: a pad is one key press, frozen.

Pitch and group

1Selected
The number and name of the selected pad.
2Pitch
The note the pad plays, from C-1 to G9, chosen in submenus by octave. The pad's Octave and Tune still apply.
3Group

Groups: one voice

Send to pad

Send to pad turns a sound made on the keyboard into a pad.

  1. In keyboard mode, set up the sound and play the note the pad should have. Last note beside the button shows it.
  2. Click Send to pad. The sixteen pads appear with their names and pitches.
  3. Click the pad to replace 1. {{APP_NAME}} asks 2 whether to replace it, naming the pad and the pitch.
  4. Click Replace (or press Return) to copy; Cancel (or Esc) keeps the pad as it was.

The pad takes every knob, switch and amount of the keyboard's sound, its program, the preset it came from if any, and, as its pitch, the last note played (C2 if none was). It keeps its group.

Presets

A preset is one sound saved as a file: the program it started from and every knob, switch and amount, Octave included. You can load it into the keyboard or into any pad, as often as you like. A pad's pitch and group are not part of it.

1The Preset menu
In the header, beside Program. Its title is the preset the sound the knobs show came from, or “—”. The menu looks into the folder each time it opens, so a preset just saved by the plug-in in a host, or copied into the folder, is there.
2Loading a preset
Click a preset to load it into the sound the knobs show: the keyboard's in keyboard mode, the selected pad's in pad mode. The other sounds stay as they are. The preset in use is ticked.
3Save as…
Saves the sound the knobs show as a preset (see Saving a preset).
4Delete
A submenu of your presets. {{APP_NAME}} asks “Delete “name”? It goes to the Trash.”: Delete moves the file to the Trash, Cancel (or Return, or Esc) keeps it. A sound that came from the deleted preset keeps its knobs.

Saving a preset

  1. Set up the sound, then choose Preset › Save as….
  2. Type a name 1. The field starts with the name of the preset in use, if there is one. Under it {{APP_NAME}} says what is stored 2.
  3. Click Save 3 or press Return. Cancel or Esc saves nothing.

A name can have up to 60 characters. Spaces at its ends are dropped, and “/” and “:” become “-”. If a preset of that name exists already (capitals do not count), {{APP_NAME}} asks “Replace “name”?”: Replace saves over it, Cancel takes you back to the name. Sounds that came from the old version keep their knobs, but a double-click now takes a knob to the new version's value.

The preset in use

Each sound, the keyboard's and every pad's, remembers the preset it came from. The menu's title and its tick show it, a pad shows its name, and a double-click takes a knob back to the preset's value. Turning knobs keeps the link. Loading a program ends it, and the title shows “—” again; so does deleting the preset's file, after which a double-click goes to the program's value.

Where presets are kept

Each preset is a file named after it, with the extension .{{APP_LOWER}}, in ~/Library/Application Support/{{APP}}/Presets. The app and the plug-in use the same folder, so a preset saved in one is in the other's menu too. You can copy the files to another Mac, or rename them in the Finder. {{APP_NAME}} makes the folder when you save your first preset.

Kits

A kit is all sixteen pads saved as one file: each pad's sound, pitch and group, and the preset each sound came from. Loading a kit replaces all sixteen pads at once; the keyboard's sound and the mode stay as they are.

1Kit
The menu beside the pads, in pad mode. Its title is always “Load or save”: once a kit is loaded, its pads are edited one by one, so no kit is ticked.
2Your kits
Click one to load it into the pads. A pad whose preset is still in the Presets folder is linked to it again.
3Default kit
Brings back the kit {{APP_NAME}} starts with (see The default kit).
4Save kit as…
Saves the sixteen pads as a kit. Naming works as for a preset (see Saving a preset).
5Delete kit
A submenu of your kits. After you confirm, the kit's file goes to the Trash.

Kits are files with the extension .{{APP_LOWER}}kit in ~/Library/Application Support/{{APP}}/Kits, shared by the app and the plug-in like presets.

LFO and VEL amounts

Two LFOs and the velocity of each note can move the knobs for you. Every knob they can move has an amount for each of them: how far that source moves it, as a part of the knob's travel, from −100 to +100 %. The amounts belong to the sound, like the knobs, so every pad has its own: on each pad you choose what its velocity and its LFOs move. They all start at 0, in every program.

Setting an amount
Click 1 LFO 1, LFO 2 or VEL in the header; the button lights up in its own colour. The knobs now show that source's amounts below them 2: drag a knob up for a positive amount, down for a negative one. An amount within 1 % of zero is zero. Double-click a knob to set its amount back to 0. Click the button again to get the knobs back.
Seeing it
A knob a source moves shows a thin arc in that source's colour inside its value arc 3, also when the buttons are off, so you see what is moving. The knob's pointer stays where you put it.
What steps back
Knobs and switches a source cannot move are dimmed while its button is on 4.

LFOs

An LFO swings a knob either way from where it stands, by its amount of the knob's travel, and never past the knob's ends. A negative amount turns the movement round. Both LFOs can move the same knob; their movements add up. The LFOs' rates, shapes and start are in Master; each voice runs LFOs of its own, and with LFO Start = Hit they start anew with every note. With Tune at 0, an amount of 50 % already swings the pitch a whole semitone (100 cents) either way.

The LFOs can move Volume, Pan and Tune.

VEL

The knob is where a note at full velocity puts it. A softer note moves it away by amount × (1 − velocity) of its travel, where velocity runs from 0 to 1 (MIDI 127 is 1). A positive amount means “the harder the note, the higher the value”: with LFO 1 Rate at +40 %, a note at half velocity stands the LFO 1 Rate knob 20 % of its travel lower, so soft notes swing slower. A negative amount works the other way round. The velocity is fixed for the note; LFO movements add to it.

Volume is the exception. Its VEL amount scales the level instead, by 1 − amount × (1 − velocity): the classic velocity-to-level. At +100 % a note at half velocity gets half the gain (−6 dB), on top of what Vel Sens does; at 0 % the velocity changes the level only through Vel Sens.

The velocity can move Volume, Pan, Tune, LFO 1 Rate and LFO 2 Rate. The switches, Octave, Bend Up and Bend Down have no VEL amount, and neither has Vel Sens: it is the velocity's own knob.

MIDI controllers

Every knob and switch of the keyboard's sound can follow a MIDI controller (a CC): a knob, fader or pedal of your MIDI keyboard or controller. Each comes with a standard controller (see the table below), so a controller that sends those numbers works at once, and any of them can learn another.

1Showing the controllers
Click MIDI in the header; it lights up. Each knob shows its controller below it instead of its value 2, and each switch in place of its choices 3: “CC 7”, or “—” for none. Click MIDI again to get the values back.
4Learning a controller
Click a knob or switch: a dashed ring appears and it says “move a control”. Move a knob or fader of your controller: the first controller that moves is taken, and the control goes where that controller stands. Click the control again to stop listening; turning MIDI off, changing the mode, switching to another app or closing the window stops it too.
The standard controller
Double-click a knob or switch to give it its standard controller back.
The secondary-click menu
Control-click a knob or switch (or click it with two fingers): No controller leaves it without one, Back to CC n (its standard number) gives the standard one back. Every control back to its standard controller, in this menu and in the MIDI button's, resets them all.
  • One controller moves one control. A controller learnt by one control is taken from the control that had it.
  • A controller puts its control where its value says: 0 is the bottom of the travel, 127 the top. On a knob whose middle is 0 (Pan, Tune, Octave), 64 is exactly the middle. A switch spreads its choices over 0–127.
  • Controllers work on every MIDI channel; the app takes them on the channels chosen in Settings.
  • Controllers move the keyboard's sound, in pad mode too. The pads and the LFO and VEL amounts have none, so with MIDI on in pad mode the controls are dimmed and show “—”.
  • A sounding note follows a controller as it follows a turn of the knob (see Keyboard mode), from its next control step, a fraction of a millisecond later.
  • The controllers belong to the app or the plug-in, not to a sound: loading a program, a preset or a kit leaves them alone. The app keeps them in its settings, so they are there after a relaunch. The plug-in keeps them with the host's project, each instance its own.
  • In the plug-in, a knob a controller moves is reported to the host as if you had turned it.

Settings

All devices (the default), None, or one connected keyboard or controller. A chosen device that is unplugged stays in the list as “not connected”; the app listens to it again as soon as it is back.

The Audio Unit plug-in

  1. In the app, open Settings and click Install Audio Unit. This copies {{APP}}.component into ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components.
  2. Restart your host or let it rescan its plug-ins.
  3. The instrument appears as {{APP_NAME}} by Jiri Pisa among the Audio Unit instruments.

The plug-in's window is the app's, without the gear: the same header, knobs, keyboard, pads and Send to pad. The keys and pads on the screen play the plug-in directly.

Automation
The host sees Turning a knob in keyboard mode can be recorded as automation, and automation moves the knobs. The pads, their pitches and groups and the mode are not parameters: automation reaches the keyboard's sound only. A parameter keeps its number in the host from version to version, so automation recorded with this version still finds its knob after an update.
Presets
The programs are the plug-in's factory presets; they load into the keyboard's sound. Your own presets are in the window's Preset menu, shared with the app (see Presets). When one is loaded into the keyboard, the host shows its name as the plug-in's preset; choosing a factory preset in the host ends that link.
Saved with the project
The keyboard's sound and its program, the mode, all sixteen pads (sound, pitch and group), the preset each sound came from, the MIDI controllers and the selected pad. A pad edit marks the project as changed half a second after you stop, or when you close the window. The plug-in's window opens on the pad selected last.
Latency
Below 88.2 kHz {{APP_NAME}} runs its voices at twice the host's rate, which delays the sound by 29 samples (0.6 ms at 48 kHz). The plug-in reports this to the host, which compensates. From 88.2 kHz up the latency is 0.
Output
Stereo. On a mono track the plug-in mixes both sides into one, so pads panned hard right are still heard.

After updating the app, click Install Audio Unit again to update the plug-in.

Updates

At every launch {{APP_NAME}} asks whether a newer version exists and, if so, offers it; nothing is installed without your OK. {{APP_NAME}} › Check for Updates… asks right away.
